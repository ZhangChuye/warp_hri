Human Perception of Whole-⁠Body Robot Motion

Comparing WARP and Mink-Based Retargeting

Chuye Zhang, Jianuo Qiu, Shaoyu Zeng, Xinchen Yin

Georgia Institute of Technology

Code WARP Paper WARP Project Mink

Motivation

Human motion can be retargeted to robots. But matching an end-effector trajectory does not tell us how that motion feels to people.

  1. Research question

    We compare two offline human-to-robot retargeting methods, WARP and MINK-TE, and ask: which motions look more trustworthy, comfortable, natural, competent, and easy to understand?

  2. Study

    Participants evaluate robot behaviors across a diverse set of manipulation tasks, using both simulation and real-world recordings.

  3. Goal

    Move beyond task-space accuracy and understand how retargeted robot motion is perceived by humans.

Retargeting Methods

Both methods follow the same human demonstration with the robot’s hands. They differ in how the rest of the body (torso, elbows, overall posture) is resolved, and that difference is what participants see.

Closed-form retargeting

WARP

Matches the palm pose exactly as a hard constraint, then uses the remaining freedom to preserve the human’s shoulder–elbow–wrist posture. Joint angles follow analytically, giving one unique solution per frame.

Optimization-based IK

MINK-TE

Weighted inverse kinematics built on the Mink library: palm-pose tracking combined with torso-orientation and elbow-swivel objectives. MINK-EF is the variant that tracks the end effector only.

Robot Platform

The robot in our study is the Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1, a wheeled humanoid whose kinematics mirror the human upper body. Like a person, it is kinematically redundant: the same hand pose can be reached with many different arm and torso postures. Each retargeting method has to choose one, and that choice is what observers see.
