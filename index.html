Human Perception of Whole-⁠Body Robot Motion

Comparing WARP and Mink-Based Retargeting

Georgia Institute of Technology

WARP Paper

Motivation

  1. Background

    Offline human-to-robot motion retargeting converts human demonstrations into robot movements, typically with the goal of reproducing task-space behavior, such as end-effector trajectories, so that the robot achieves the same manipulation effect on the environment.

  2. Gap

    Such retargeted trajectories can therefore serve as offline robot demonstrations for downstream behavior cloning. However, task-space tracking accuracy alone does not establish whether people trust the robot, feel comfortable around it, or perceive its motion as natural and understandable.

  3. This project

    This project compares user perceptions of robot motions generated by WARP and Mink-based baselines across several HRI dimensions. A diverse set of tasks from both simulation and real-world recordings will be used as study stimuli.

Retargeting Methods

All methods follow the same human demonstration with the robot’s hands. They differ in how the rest of the body (torso, elbows, overall posture) is resolved, and that difference is what participants see.

Closed-form retargeting

WARP

Matches the palm pose exactly as a hard constraint, then uses the remaining freedom to preserve the human’s shoulder–elbow–wrist posture. Joint angles follow analytically, giving one unique solution per frame.

Optimization-based IK

Mink-based baselines

Weighted inverse kinematics built on the Mink library: palm-pose tracking with optional torso-orientation and elbow-swivel objectives. MINK-EF tracks the end effector only; MINK-TE adds the torso and elbow terms.

Robot Platform

The robot in our study is the Rainbow Robotics RB-Y1, a wheeled humanoid whose kinematics mirror the human upper body. Like a person, it is kinematically redundant: the same hand pose can be reached with many different arm and torso postures. Each retargeting method has to choose one, and that choice is what observers see.
